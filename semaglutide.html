Semaglutide: The Complete Guide

Everything you need to know about the active ingredient behind Ozempic, Wegovy, and Rybelsus — the most widely prescribed GLP-1 medication in the world.

Medically Reviewed·Updated February 2026·15 min read

What is Semaglutide?

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist developed by Novo Nordisk. It mimics the naturally occurring GLP-1 hormone in your gut, but with a much longer duration of action. A single dose remains active in your body for approximately one week, making it far more potent and practical than the hormone your body produces on its own.

First approved by the FDA in 2017 for type 2 diabetes (as Ozempic), semaglutide gained massive attention when higher-dose formulations showed dramatic weight loss results, leading to Wegovy's approval for weight management in 2021.

How Semaglutide Works

Semaglutide works through multiple mechanisms simultaneously:

  • Brain signaling — Acts on appetite-regulating centres in the hypothalamus, reducing hunger and increasing satiety.
  • Gastric motility — Slows stomach emptying, keeping you full longer after meals.
  • Insulin regulation — Stimulates glucose-dependent insulin secretion, reducing hypoglycemia risk.
  • Glucagon suppression — Reduces glucagon secretion, helping lower blood sugar.
  • "Food noise" reduction — Many patients describe a dramatic reduction in constant food-related thoughts and cravings.
Why weekly? Natural GLP-1 has a half-life of just 2 minutes. Semaglutide is chemically modified (94% structurally similar to human GLP-1) to bind to albumin in your blood, giving it a half-life of approximately 7 days.

Clinical Trial Results

STEP Trials (Weight Management)

  • STEP 1: Average weight loss of 14.9% over 68 weeks vs. 2.4% with placebo.
  • STEP 2 (type 2 diabetes): Average weight loss of 9.6%.
  • STEP 3 (with behavioural therapy): Average weight loss of 16%.
  • STEP 5 (2-year study): ~15% weight loss sustained over 2 years of continued treatment.

SELECT Trial (Cardiovascular)

The landmark SELECT trial demonstrated semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 20% in overweight/obese adults with established cardiovascular disease — even without diabetes. This shifted semaglutide from "weight loss drug" to a medication with proven heart benefits.

Key finding: About one-third of STEP trial participants lost 20%+ of their body weight — results previously only achievable through bariatric surgery.

Side Effects

Very Common (>10% of patients)

  • Nausea (44%) — worst during dose escalations
  • Diarrhoea (30%)
  • Vomiting (24%)
  • Constipation (24%)
  • Abdominal pain (20%)

Common (1–10%)

  • Headache, fatigue, dizziness
  • Injection site reactions
  • Gastroesophageal reflux
  • Hair thinning (related to rapid weight loss)

Serious (Rare)

  • Pancreatitis
  • Gallbladder disease
  • Thyroid C-cell tumours (boxed warning based on animal studies)
  • Acute kidney injury (usually from dehydration)
Pro tip: Smaller, more frequent meals, avoiding fatty foods, and staying well hydrated can significantly reduce GI side effects. Most peak in the first 2–3 weeks of each dose increase then subside.

Who Should (and Shouldn't) Take Semaglutide

Good Candidates

  • Adults with BMI ≥30 (for Wegovy)
  • Adults with BMI ≥27 with a weight-related comorbidity (for Wegovy)
  • Adults with type 2 diabetes needing additional glycaemic control
  • Adults with established cardiovascular disease and obesity

Contraindications

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma
  • Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2
  • Known hypersensitivity to semaglutide
  • Pregnancy or planning to become pregnant (discontinue 2+ months before)
