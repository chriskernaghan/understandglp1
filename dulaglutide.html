Dulaglutide (Trulicity): The Complete Guide

The user-friendly weekly GLP-1 for type 2 diabetes — known for its simple auto-inject pen, proven cardiovascular benefits, and reliable blood sugar control.

Medically Reviewed·Updated February 2026·11 min read

What is Dulaglutide?

Dulaglutide (brand name Trulicity) is a once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist developed by Eli Lilly. Approved by the FDA in 2014, it became one of the most prescribed GLP-1 medications globally thanks to its remarkably simple injection device — a ready-to-use pen with a hidden needle that auto-injects at the press of a button.

Unlike semaglutide and tirzepatide, dulaglutide is not approved for weight management as a standalone indication. It does produce modest weight loss as a secondary benefit, and remains a mainstay of diabetes treatment.

The ease factor: Trulicity's pen was designed for needle-phobic patients. The needle is pre-attached and hidden — patients click a button and the injection happens automatically. This made it one of the most popular GLP-1s for people new to injectables.

How Dulaglutide Works

  • Glucose-dependent insulin secretion — Stimulates insulin when blood sugar is elevated, with minimal hypoglycemia risk.
  • Glucagon suppression — Reduces the hormone that raises blood sugar.
  • Slowed gastric emptying — Smooths post-meal blood sugar spikes.
  • Modest appetite reduction — Less pronounced than semaglutide or tirzepatide.

Dulaglutide is fused to a modified immunoglobulin (IgG4) Fc fragment, which slows clearance and enables weekly dosing. This larger size may limit blood-brain barrier crossing, explaining the less pronounced appetite suppression.

Clinical Trial Results

AWARD Trials (Diabetes)

  • A1C reductions of 1.1–1.6% from baseline
  • Superior to sitagliptin, insulin glargine, and metformin monotherapy
  • Weight loss of 1.5–3 kg (3–7 lbs) over 26–52 weeks

REWIND Trial (Cardiovascular)

REWIND studied a broader population than most CV outcome trials — including patients without established cardiovascular disease (only 31% had prior CV events). Dulaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 12%, suggesting heart protection even in lower-risk diabetic patients.

Why REWIND matters: Most GLP-1 CV trials enrolled high-risk patients. REWIND's broader population suggests dulaglutide's cardiovascular benefits extend to a wider range of type 2 diabetes patients.

Side Effects

Dulaglutide's side effect profile is generally milder than semaglutide.

Common (>5%)

  • Nausea (~12–21%) — significantly lower than semaglutide (~44%)
  • Diarrhoea (~8–13%)
  • Vomiting (~6–12%)
  • Abdominal pain (~6–9%)
  • Decreased appetite (~5–9%)

Serious (Rare)

  • Pancreatitis
  • Thyroid C-cell tumours (boxed warning)
  • Hypoglycemia (with insulin/sulfonylureas)
  • Acute kidney injury
Tolerability advantage: Trulicity's lower nausea and vomiting rates make it a good option for patients sensitive to GI side effects or who couldn't tolerate other GLP-1 medications.

Cost & Insurance

  • Trulicity: ~$900–$1,000/month (list price)

As a well-established diabetes medication, Trulicity has broader insurance coverage than newer weight-focused GLP-1s. Most commercial and many Medicare Part D plans cover it. Eli Lilly's savings card can reduce costs to $25/month for eligible patients.

Who is Dulaglutide Best For?

  • Patients focused on diabetes control — excellent A1C reduction with simpler titration
  • Needle-averse patients — hidden-needle auto-inject pen is the most user-friendly device
  • Patients who don't tolerate other GLP-1s — lower GI side effect rates
  • Cardiovascular risk reduction — REWIND data supports heart protection
  • Insurance-driven decisions — often better covered than Ozempic or Mounjaro
