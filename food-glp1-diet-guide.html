Nutrition Guide

The GLP-1 Diet: What to Eat (and Avoid) for Best Results

Feb 20, 2026·14 min read

Here's the paradox of GLP-1 medications: your appetite is dramatically reduced, but your nutritional needs haven't changed — and in some ways, they've actually increased. When you're eating 40–60% less food than before, every meal needs to work harder. Getting this right is the difference between losing weight healthily and losing weight in a way that leaves you depleted, weak, and nutritionally deficient.

This guide covers the principles, the priorities, and the practical strategies for eating well on a GLP-1 medication.

The protein priority

If there's one nutritional message to take from this entire article, it's this: protein first, always. Research consistently shows that 20–40% of weight lost on GLP-1 medications can be lean muscle mass rather than fat. The primary defense against this is adequate protein intake combined with resistance training.

The target for most GLP-1 patients is 0.7–1.0 grams of protein per pound of your ideal body weight per day. For many people, that means 80–120g of protein daily. When your appetite is suppressed, hitting that number requires intentionality.

Practical protein strategies

  • Eat protein first — At every meal, start with your protein source before touching anything else. If you get full before finishing, at least you've prioritised the most important macronutrient.
  • Front-load your day — Many GLP-1 patients find their appetite is lowest in the evening. Get the bulk of your protein in at breakfast and lunch when you can still tolerate eating.
  • Use protein supplements strategically — A high-quality protein shake (30–40g protein) can fill gaps on days when eating solid food feels impossible. Look for options that are low in fat and sugar to minimise GI irritation.
  • Choose dense sources — Greek yoghurt, cottage cheese, eggs, chicken breast, turkey mince, tinned tuna, and whey protein are all high in protein relative to volume.
The 30g rule: Research suggests your body can optimally utilise about 30–40g of protein per meal for muscle protein synthesis. Rather than trying to get all your protein in one sitting, aim for 3–4 meals or snacks each containing 25–35g of protein.

Foods to prioritise

Beyond protein, focus on nutrient density — foods that deliver maximum vitamins, minerals, and fibre per calorie.

  • Lean proteins: chicken, turkey, fish, eggs, Greek yoghurt, cottage cheese, tofu, lentils
  • Non-starchy vegetables: leafy greens, broccoli, cauliflower, courgettes, peppers, tomatoes
  • Complex carbohydrates: sweet potatoes, quinoa, brown rice, oats (in moderate portions)
  • Healthy fats: avocado, olive oil, nuts, seeds (in small amounts — fat can slow gastric emptying further and trigger nausea)
  • Hydrating foods: soups, smoothies, watermelon, cucumber — especially helpful when solid food is hard to manage

Foods to avoid (or limit)

Certain foods are particularly problematic on GLP-1 medications — not because they're "bad" in general, but because they interact poorly with the drug's effects on your digestive system.

  • High-fat, greasy foods: Fried foods, heavy cream sauces, and fatty meats dramatically slow an already-slow stomach and are the number one nausea trigger.
  • Very sugary foods: Sweets, fizzy drinks, and desserts can cause "dumping syndrome" — rapid blood sugar fluctuations that cause nausea, sweating, and dizziness.
  • Carbonated drinks: The gas from sparkling water and fizzy drinks can cause significant bloating and discomfort with slowed gastric emptying.
  • Large portions of anything: Even healthy food will make you miserable if you eat too much. Your capacity is genuinely reduced.
  • Alcohol: Many patients report dramatically reduced alcohol tolerance. GLP-1 medications slow alcohol absorption, intensifying its effects. Start very slowly and cautiously.

A sample day of eating

Here's what a good day might look like on a GLP-1 medication, targeting approximately 1,200–1,400 calories and 100g+ protein:

Breakfast (7:30 AM): Two scrambled eggs with smoked salmon on half a slice of wholemeal toast. Greek yoghurt with a handful of berries. ~35g protein, ~350 calories.

Lunch (12:30 PM): Chicken and vegetable soup with lentils. Small side salad with olive oil dressing. ~30g protein, ~350 calories.

Afternoon snack (3:30 PM): Protein shake blended with frozen banana and peanut butter. ~30g protein, ~250 calories.

Dinner (6:30 PM): Grilled white fish with roasted vegetables and a small portion of quinoa. ~25g protein, ~300 calories.

Daily total: ~120g protein, ~1,250 calories.

Supplements to consider

When you're eating significantly less, certain nutritional gaps become nearly inevitable. Discuss these with your provider:

  • Multivitamin — A good quality daily multivitamin covers basic gaps.
  • Vitamin D — Commonly deficient, especially in northern climates.
  • Iron — Particularly for women, as reduced food intake can make iron deficiency more likely.
  • B12 — May be reduced with lower food intake and altered gut function.
  • Fibre supplement — Constipation is common on GLP-1s. A psyllium husk supplement can help.
  • Biotin — May help with hair thinning associated with rapid weight loss.

The mindset shift

Perhaps the biggest adjustment isn't about specific foods — it's about reframing your relationship with eating. On a GLP-1, eating becomes less about pleasure and emotional comfort and more about fuel and nutrition. This can feel like a loss at first, but most patients eventually describe it as liberating.

You're not on a diet. You're on a medication that has changed your hunger signals, and you're choosing to fuel your body well within that new reality. That's a fundamentally different — and much healthier — framework than any diet you've tried before.
