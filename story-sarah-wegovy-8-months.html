"The Food Noise Disappeared — and So Did 47 Pounds"

Feb 24, 2026·12 min read

Sarah M., 48, is a teacher from Manchester. She started Wegovy in June 2025 after decades of yo-yo dieting. This is her story in her own words, lightly edited for clarity.

Before: thirty years of food noise

I've been overweight since I was a teenager. I've done every diet you can think of — Weight Watchers, Slimming World, keto, intermittent fasting, juice cleanses, the lot. I'd lose two stone, regain three. Lose one, regain two. The cycle was exhausting, humiliating, and completely demoralising.

But the worst part wasn't the weight itself. It was the constant, relentless thinking about food. I'd wake up and immediately start planning what I'd eat that day. By mid-morning, I'd be thinking about lunch. After lunch, I'd be thinking about dinner. After dinner, I'd be thinking about snacks. It never stopped. I didn't know this had a name — "food noise" — until I started reading about GLP-1 medications.

Making the decision

My GP brought up Wegovy during a routine appointment in May 2025. My BMI was 38, I had borderline high blood pressure, and my blood sugar was creeping into pre-diabetic territory. She explained it was a weekly injection, that it would suppress my appetite, and that the results from clinical trials were significant.

I was sceptical. After 30 years of failed diets, I'd developed a deep cynicism about anything that promised weight loss. But my GP was straightforward: this wasn't a diet. It was a medication that changed the hormonal signals driving my eating behaviour. That framing made a difference.

Month one: the shock

I took my first injection on a Tuesday evening. By Thursday, something had shifted. I ate breakfast and simply... forgot about food until lunchtime. This had literally never happened to me as an adult. The silence in my head where the food noise used to be was almost disorienting.

The nausea was real — maybe a 4 out of 10 for the first week. I stuck to small, bland meals and it was manageable. By week three, the nausea had mostly passed.

Sarah's tip: "Keep ginger tea and plain crackers stocked for the first few weeks of each dose increase. And eat slowly — your stomach is emptier than you think, and the signal to stop comes much sooner now."

Months two through five: steady progress

The weight came off steadily — about 2–3 lbs per week initially, then slowing to about 1–1.5 lbs per week as the months went on. But the weight was almost secondary. The real transformation was psychological.

I could walk past a bakery without going in. I could keep biscuits in the house without eating the entire packet. I could sit through a film without thinking about what was in the fridge. These might sound like small things, but for someone who spent 30 years controlled by food thoughts, they were nothing short of miraculous.

The challenges

It hasn't all been smooth. I had significant constipation around month three that required a fibre supplement. My hair started thinning around month four — my doctor said this was related to the rate of weight loss, not the drug itself, and recommended biotin and protein supplementation.

Socially, it's been complicated. Some friends have been incredibly supportive. Others have made comments about "taking the easy way out" or "cheating." I've had to develop thicker skin and remind myself that this is a medical treatment, not a shortcut.

Eight months in: 47 pounds down

As I write this, I've lost 47 lbs — just over three stone. My blood pressure is normal. My blood sugar is back in the healthy range. I've gone from a size 22 to a size 16. But the number I care about most is zero: the number of hours I now spend per day obsessing about food.

I'm not naive about the future. I know the research shows weight regain is common if you stop the medication. I'm planning to stay on it long-term, and I'm working on building exercise habits and better nutrition patterns that will serve me regardless.

What I wish I'd known

  • Start tracking protein from day one. When your appetite drops, it's easy to just eat less of everything — but you need to protect your muscle mass. I wish I'd started focusing on protein immediately rather than month four.
  • The nausea is temporary. Don't let the first few weeks scare you off. It genuinely gets better.
  • Find your people. Online communities of people on GLP-1 medications have been invaluable for practical tips and emotional support.
  • It's not cheating. Nobody says blood pressure medication is "cheating." This is the same thing — treating a medical condition with medicine.
