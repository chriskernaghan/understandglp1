"Trulicity Gave Me My A1C Back — and My Confidence"

Feb 8, 2026·8 min read
At a glance: David T., 58, was diagnosed with type 2 diabetes in his late 40s and spent nearly a decade struggling with oral medications that weren't doing enough. After starting Trulicity (dulaglutide), his A1C dropped from 9.1 to 6.4 over 18 months. He lost 22 lbs without trying to, and for the first time in years, stopped dreading his blood work appointments.

A decade of feeling like I was failing

I was diagnosed with type 2 diabetes at 48. Looking back, I probably had it for years before that — I just avoided going to the doctor. When I finally did, my A1C was 8.6 and my GP put me on metformin straight away.

Metformin did something. It brought my numbers down a bit — A1C hovered around 7.8 for a couple of years. But it never got me where I needed to be, and the digestive side effects were miserable. I was on the maximum dose and still not hitting my target.

Over the next few years, my doctor added a sulfonylurea, then swapped it for a different one when the first stopped working. My A1C crept back up. By the time I was 56, I was on two oral medications, my A1C was 9.1, and my doctor was having the insulin conversation.

Every blood test felt like getting a school report. And I was always failing. No matter what I did, the numbers kept getting worse.

The worst part wasn't the numbers themselves. It was the shame. I felt like I was doing something wrong — eating too much, not exercising enough, not trying hard enough. Every three-month blood test felt like a verdict on my character. My wife could tell when results day was coming because I'd go quiet for days beforehand.

Why Trulicity, not Ozempic

My doctor suggested a GLP-1 medication before moving to insulin. I'd heard of Ozempic from the news — hard not to — but she recommended Trulicity instead. Her reasoning was straightforward: for my situation, which was primarily about blood sugar control rather than weight loss, Trulicity had a strong evidence base and she'd had good results with her other diabetes patients on it.

About Trulicity: Trulicity (dulaglutide) is a GLP-1 receptor agonist made by Eli Lilly, approved for type 2 diabetes management. It's a once-weekly injection, like Ozempic, but uses a different active ingredient. While it's not as widely discussed as Ozempic or Mounjaro in the media, it's one of the most prescribed GLP-1 medications for diabetes globally. It's not currently approved specifically for weight loss.

I'll be honest — I was relieved it wasn't insulin. That felt like a line I wasn't ready to cross. The idea of a weekly injection that could improve my blood sugar without the complexity of insulin dosing was appealing. My doctor was clear that this wasn't a guarantee, and that if it didn't work, insulin was still on the table. But it was worth trying.

Getting started

The Trulicity pen is probably the easiest injection device I've ever seen. You click it against your stomach or thigh, press a button, wait for the click, and you're done. The needle is hidden — you never see it. For someone who'd been dreading injections, it was a non-event.

I started on 0.75mg. Side effects were mild — some nausea for the first week or two, a bit of an upset stomach. Nothing compared to what metformin had put me through for years. By week three, I felt completely normal.

The first thing I noticed wasn't my blood sugar. It was that I was less hungry between meals. I wasn't ravenous by mid-afternoon anymore. I stopped reaching for biscuits with my tea out of habit. I didn't set out to lose weight, but I was naturally eating less without thinking about it.

The numbers that changed everything

My first A1C test after starting Trulicity was at three months. I'd been dreading it, as usual. My wife drove me to the surgery and waited in the car.

7.4.

Down from 9.1. In three months. On a single medication change.

I sat in the doctor's office and didn't know what to say. She smiled and said, "This is what was supposed to happen. Your diabetes wasn't a willpower problem — you just needed the right tool."

That sentence changed something in me. For a decade I'd been blaming myself. And here was this medication doing what all my guilt and effort couldn't.

The side effects — honest version

I've seen horror stories online about GLP-1 side effects, so I want to be upfront about my experience. Trulicity was gentle for me. That doesn't mean it will be for everyone, but here's exactly what I dealt with:

  • Weeks 1–3: Mild nausea, mostly in the mornings. Manageable — I ate a small breakfast and it passed. Nothing close to what I'd experienced on metformin.
  • Week 4 onward (0.75mg): Side effects essentially gone. Felt completely normal.
  • Dose increase to 1.5mg (month 3): Slight return of nausea for about a week, then settled again. Some constipation that I managed by drinking more water and eating more fibre.
  • Months 6–18: No ongoing side effects. Injection site occasionally itches for an hour. That's it.

The one thing I will flag: I had a couple of episodes of mild hypoglycaemia in the first few months, which my doctor said was likely because of the combination with the sulfonylurea. She discontinued the sulfonylurea after my six-month results came back, and I haven't had an episode since.

What it's actually like living on Trulicity

The day-to-day reality is remarkably unremarkable — and I mean that as the highest compliment. Every Thursday evening, I do my injection. It takes about 10 seconds. I put the pen back in the fridge. And that's it until next Thursday.

My appetite is lower but not gone. I still enjoy food — I still look forward to meals, I still eat out with my wife, I still have a Sunday roast. I just eat less. The portions that used to feel normal now feel like more than enough. I leave food on my plate sometimes, which is something I never did before.

For the first time in ten years, I don't dread my blood work. I actually look forward to it — because I know the numbers will be good. That's a feeling I never thought I'd have.

The weight loss has been gradual and steady — 22 lbs over 18 months. I wasn't trying to lose weight; it just happened as a consequence of eating less. My doctor said that's typical with Trulicity — the weight loss is more modest than what you see with Ozempic or Mounjaro, but it's real and it helps with the diabetes management.

The conversation about Ozempic

About a year in, I asked my doctor whether I should switch to Ozempic or Mounjaro. By that point they were everywhere in the news, and I wondered if I was on the "wrong" medication.

She was reassuring. She said Trulicity was doing exactly what it needed to do for me. My A1C was 6.5, my blood sugar was stable, my side effects were minimal. Switching to chase a slightly bigger number on the weight loss side didn't make sense when everything was working. If my A1C started climbing again or if I needed more weight loss support in the future, we'd revisit it.

That made sense to me. There's a lot of noise about GLP-1 medications right now, and it's easy to feel like you're missing out if you're not on the latest, most talked-about one. But the best medication is the one that works for you, not the one with the biggest headlines.

What I wish I'd known earlier

  • Diabetes isn't a character flaw. I spent a decade blaming myself for numbers that were largely driven by biology. GLP-1 medications work on your hormones, not your willpower. If oral medications aren't enough, that's not a failure — it's just biology.
  • Trulicity deserves more attention. It's not as famous as Ozempic, but for straightforward type 2 diabetes management, it's a serious medication with a strong track record. Don't dismiss it because it's not trending on social media.
  • The injection is nothing. I was anxious about it for weeks before starting. The pen is so simple and the needle so small that my wife didn't believe I'd actually done it the first time. If fear of injections is holding you back, I promise — it's nothing.
  • Ask about your medication combination. The hypoglycaemia episodes I had were caused by the interaction between Trulicity and my sulfonylurea, not Trulicity alone. Once my doctor adjusted the other medications, the problem went away entirely.
  • Give it time. My A1C didn't reach its best level until month 12. The improvement was gradual and steady. If you're not seeing dramatic results at month two, that doesn't mean it isn't working.
Trulicity vs. the newer GLP-1s: Trulicity, Ozempic, and Mounjaro are all GLP-1 receptor agonists, but they have different active ingredients and different approved uses. Trulicity (dulaglutide) is approved specifically for type 2 diabetes. Ozempic (semaglutide) is approved for diabetes with weight loss as a common benefit. Mounjaro (tirzepatide) is a dual-agonist approved for diabetes. Your doctor can help determine which is the best fit based on your specific situation and goals.

Where I am now

Eighteen months in and I feel like a different person. Not physically — though 22 lbs lighter and an A1C of 6.4 is nothing to dismiss. The real change is mental. I don't carry the weight of failure around anymore. I go to my blood tests without dread. I talk about my diabetes openly instead of hiding it.

My doctor recently reduced my metformin dose because my numbers are so well controlled. That's a sentence I never imagined writing. For the first time in a decade, I'm moving in the right direction — not because I found some hidden reserve of willpower, but because I finally got the right tool for the job.

If you're sitting where I was — on multiple oral medications, watching your A1C climb, feeling like you're losing a battle — please talk to your doctor about GLP-1 options. It doesn't have to be Trulicity. It doesn't have to be Ozempic. But it might be the thing that changes everything.
