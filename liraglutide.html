Liraglutide: The Complete Guide

The original daily GLP-1 behind Victoza and Saxenda — with over a decade of real-world safety data and the longest track record of any GLP-1 medication.

Medically Reviewed·Updated February 2026·12 min read

What is Liraglutide?

Liraglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist developed by Novo Nordisk — the same company behind semaglutide. Approved in 2010 for type 2 diabetes (Victoza) and 2014 for weight management (Saxenda), liraglutide paved the way for the GLP-1 revolution.

While newer agents have surpassed it in weight loss efficacy, liraglutide remains important for patients who need extensive long-term safety data, or who prefer the faster washout of a daily injection.

Clinical Trial Results

SCALE Trials (Weight — Saxenda)

  • SCALE Obesity: Average weight loss of 8% over 56 weeks (vs. 2.6% placebo).
  • About 33% of participants achieved ≥10% body weight loss.

LEADER Trial (Cardiovascular — Victoza)

Demonstrated liraglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 13% in patients with type 2 diabetes and high CV risk — one of the first diabetes drugs with proven heart benefits.

Context: While 8% weight loss is lower than semaglutide (~15%) or tirzepatide (~22%), a 5–10% loss is associated with significant improvements in blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, and sleep apnoea.

Victoza (Diabetes)

Start at 0.6 mg/day for one week, then increase to 1.2 mg/day (standard), with optional increase to 1.8 mg/day for additional control. Can be injected any time of day.

Side Effects

Very Common (>10%)

  • Nausea (~39%)
  • Diarrhoea (~21%)
  • Constipation (~19%)
  • Vomiting (~16%)
  • Headache (~14%)

Serious (Rare)

  • Pancreatitis
  • Gallbladder disease
  • Thyroid C-cell tumours (boxed warning)
  • Acute kidney injury
Advantage of daily dosing: If you experience intolerable side effects, liraglutide clears your system within 2–3 days. Weekly injectables take 4–5 weeks to fully wash out.

Cost & Insurance

  • Victoza: ~$900–$1,000/month
  • Saxenda: ~$1,300–$1,400/month

Insurance coverage for Victoza (diabetes) is generally better than for newer agents. Some providers consider liraglutide a good "starter" GLP-1 when insurance covers it more readily.

Who is Liraglutide Best For?

  • Patients wanting a shorter-acting option — faster washout if side effects are problematic
  • Adolescents — Saxenda is FDA-approved for ages 12+ for weight management
  • Patients prioritising safety data — 15+ years of real-world post-marketing data
  • Cardiovascular risk patients — proven CV benefit from LEADER trial
  • Insurance-driven choices — often better covered than newer alternatives
