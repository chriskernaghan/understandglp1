Ozempic vs. Mounjaro in 2026: Which Should You Choose?

Feb 24, 2026·18 min read

If you're considering a GLP-1 medication for weight management or diabetes, chances are you've narrowed it down to two names: Ozempic (semaglutide) and Mounjaro (tirzepatide). They're the two most talked-about medications in the space — and for good reason. Both deliver remarkable results, but they work differently and the right choice depends on your specific situation.

This guide breaks down everything you need to know to make an informed decision, based on clinical trial data, real-world experience, and practical considerations like cost and insurance.

The fundamental difference

The most important distinction is their mechanism of action. Ozempic (and its weight-management counterpart Wegovy) contains semaglutide, a GLP-1 receptor agonist that mimics one gut hormone. Mounjaro (and Zepbound) contains tirzepatide, a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist that mimics two gut hormones simultaneously.

Think of it this way: semaglutide is pulling one lever very effectively. Tirzepatide is pulling two levers at once. The additional GIP receptor activation enhances fat metabolism, improves insulin sensitivity in fat tissue, and may help explain why tirzepatide consistently shows greater weight loss in trials.

Quick summary: Ozempic = one hormone (GLP-1). Mounjaro = two hormones (GLP-1 + GIP). Both are weekly injections. Tirzepatide generally produces more weight loss, but semaglutide has more long-term safety data and a proven cardiovascular benefit.

Weight loss: the numbers

Let's look at what the clinical trials actually showed. It's worth noting these are averages — individual results vary significantly.

In the STEP 1 trial, participants on semaglutide 2.4 mg (Wegovy) lost an average of 14.9% of their body weight over 68 weeks. About a third of participants achieved 20% or greater weight loss. In the SURMOUNT-1 trial, participants on tirzepatide 15 mg lost an average of 22.5% of their body weight over 72 weeks. Over a third achieved 25% or greater weight loss.

That's a meaningful gap — roughly 5-7 percentage points more weight loss with tirzepatide at maximum doses. However, it's important to note that not everyone reaches the maximum dose of either medication, and real-world results tend to be somewhat lower than clinical trial results.

Side effects compared

Both medications cause gastrointestinal side effects, primarily nausea, diarrhea, constipation, and vomiting. However, there are some notable differences.

Nausea rates were approximately 44% with semaglutide versus 29% with tirzepatide in their respective trials. Some researchers believe the GIP component of tirzepatide may help mitigate GLP-1-related nausea, though this hasn't been definitively proven.

Both medications carry the same boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors (based on animal studies), and both carry risks of pancreatitis and gallbladder problems. Neither is approved for use in pregnancy.

Cost and insurance

Without insurance, both medications are expensive. Ozempic runs approximately $900–$1,000 per month at list price, while Mounjaro is similar at $1,000–$1,100 per month. For weight management specifically, Wegovy lists at $1,300–$1,400 per month and Zepbound at approximately $1,000–$1,100.

Eli Lilly has been more aggressive on pricing for Zepbound, offering direct-to-consumer pricing through LillyDirect and a savings card that can bring costs down to $550/month for cash-pay patients. Novo Nordisk has responded with their own savings programs, but Lilly's pricing has generally been more competitive.

Insurance coverage varies dramatically by plan and by indication (diabetes vs. weight management). Ozempic has been on the market longer and often has better formulary placement for diabetes. Zepbound is newer but gaining coverage rapidly.

Cardiovascular benefits

This is where semaglutide currently has a significant advantage. The SELECT trial demonstrated that semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 20% in overweight and obese adults with established cardiovascular disease. This was a landmark finding that positions semaglutide as more than just a weight loss drug.

Tirzepatide's cardiovascular outcome trial is ongoing. Early data from diabetes trials is encouraging, but we don't yet have the definitive CV outcome data that semaglutide has. For patients where cardiovascular risk reduction is a primary concern, this may tip the scale toward semaglutide.

So which should you choose?

There's no universally right answer, but here's a framework to help you think through it:

  • Maximum weight loss is your priority — Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) has consistently shown greater weight loss in clinical trials.
  • You're sensitive to nausea — Tirzepatide may be better tolerated for some patients based on reported nausea rates.
  • Cardiovascular risk is a concern — Semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy) has proven cardiovascular benefits from the SELECT trial.
  • You want an oral option — Only semaglutide is available as a pill (Rybelsus), though it's only approved for diabetes, not weight management.
  • Cost is a major factor — Compare your specific insurance coverage for both. Lilly's direct pricing may be more favorable for Zepbound.
  • You want the longest safety track record — Semaglutide has been on the market since 2017; tirzepatide since 2022.
The honest answer: Many patients may try both over time. It's increasingly common for people to start on one and switch if they don't get adequate results or have tolerability issues. The medications aren't interchangeable, so switching can sometimes unlock better outcomes.

Talk to your provider

Ultimately, this decision should be made with a healthcare provider who understands your full medical history, your goals, and your insurance situation. Both medications represent genuine advances in metabolic medicine — the best one is the one that works for your specific circumstances.
