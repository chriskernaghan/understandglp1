Why You're Losing Hair on a GLP-1 (And How to Stop It)

Updated April 2026·9 min read

There are few things more alarming than taking a shower, running your hands through your hair, and coming away with a handful of loose strands. For many people on GLP-1 medications like Wegovy, Zepbound, or Ozempic, this terrifying shedding phase hits right around the three-to-four-month mark.

The immediate reaction is usually panic. Is the medication toxic? Am I going bald? Do I need to stop taking my injections?

The short answer to all three questions is no. What you're experiencing is a very well-documented, temporary biological response to rapid weight loss. It's called telogen effluvium, and understanding how it works is the first step to getting your hair back on track.

The bottom line up front: Semaglutide and tirzepatide do not directly attack your hair follicles. The hair loss is a stress response to the severe caloric deficit and the rapid shedding of body weight. It is almost always temporary.

What is Telogen Effluvium?

To understand why your hair is falling out, you have to understand how it grows. At any given moment, every hair on your head is in one of three phases:

  • Anagen (Growing Phase): Lasts for years. About 85% to 90% of your hair is currently in this phase.
  • Catagen (Transition Phase): Lasts a few weeks. The hair stops growing and detaches from the blood supply.
  • Telogen (Resting Phase): Lasts about three months. The hair sits dormant before eventually falling out to make room for a new hair.

When you put your body through a major physical stressor—like surgery, a severe illness, giving birth, or, crucially, rapid and significant weight loss—your body enters survival mode. It looks at the energy required to grow hair and decides it's a luxury it can't afford right now.

As a result, your body prematurely pushes a massive percentage of your hair (sometimes up to 30% or 40%) out of the growing phase and straight into the resting (telogen) phase. Because the resting phase lasts about three months, you won't actually see the hair fall out until three to four months after the weight loss began. The delay is why the shedding often feels so sudden and disconnected from the initial start of your diet.

How to minimize the shed and encourage regrowth

Because the hair that is falling out today actually "died" three months ago, there isn't a magical shampoo that will instantly stop the current shedding. The hair in the telogen phase has to come out. However, you can take immediate steps to signal your body that it's safe to start growing new hair (the anagen phase) right now.

1. Fix your protein intake

Hair is made almost entirely of a protein called keratin. If you aren't eating enough protein on a GLP-1, your body will absolutely not waste its scarce resources on hair growth; it will prioritize your heart, lungs, and muscles instead. You need to consistently hit high protein targets (roughly 0.8 to 1 gram of protein per pound of ideal body weight) to give your follicles the building blocks they need.

2. Check your iron and vitamin levels

The appetite suppression from GLP-1s often leads to unintentional malnutrition. You're simply eating less food, which means fewer micronutrients. Iron deficiency is one of the most common culprits of hair thinning, especially in women. Talk to your doctor about running a blood panel to check your Ferritin (iron storage), Vitamin D, Zinc, and B12 levels. If you're deficient, targeted supplementation will make a massive difference.

3. Don't crash diet

The faster you lose weight, the harder the shock to your system, and the more severe the telogen effluvium will be. While dropping 15 pounds in a month sounds exciting, it's a traumatic event for your biology. Aiming for a sustainable weight loss of 1 to 2 pounds per week gives your body time to adjust without hitting the panic button that halts hair growth.

When to see a doctor

Telogen effluvium causes diffuse thinning all over the scalp. If your hair is falling out in distinct, round, bald patches, or if your scalp is red, itchy, and flaking, this isn't standard weight-loss shedding. It could be alopecia areata, a thyroid issue, or a dermatological condition. See your primary care provider or a dermatologist for an accurate diagnosis.

The regrowth timeline

The most frustrating part of telogen effluvium is the waiting game. It requires immense patience. Once the trigger (the rapid weight loss) stabilizes, the excessive shedding typically lasts for about three to six months.

After the shedding stops, the follicles re-enter the growing phase. Because hair only grows about half an inch per month, it can take six months to a year to notice significant thickening and regain your original density. You'll likely notice short, spiky "baby hairs" around your hairline first—this is exactly what you want to see. It means the system is resetting.

The bottom line

Losing your hair is emotionally distressing, but when it happens alongside GLP-1 weight loss, it's a predictable, mechanical response to a changing body. It doesn't mean you're broken, and it doesn't mean the medication is poisoning your hair follicles.

Feed your body enough protein, supplement where you're deficient, avoid starvation-level calories, and give your hair growth cycle the time it needs to reset. It will grow back.
