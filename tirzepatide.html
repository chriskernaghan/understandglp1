Tirzepatide: The Complete Guide

The dual-action GIP/GLP-1 agonist behind Mounjaro and Zepbound — delivering the highest weight loss results of any injectable medication to date.

Medically Reviewed·Updated February 2026·14 min read

What is Tirzepatide?

Tirzepatide is a first-in-class dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist developed by Eli Lilly. Unlike semaglutide which only targets GLP-1, tirzepatide simultaneously activates both GIP and GLP-1 receptors — two key incretin hormones involved in blood sugar regulation and appetite.

Approved by the FDA in 2022 for type 2 diabetes (Mounjaro) and in 2023 for weight management (Zepbound), tirzepatide has quickly become one of the most sought-after medications due to its superior weight loss results.

How Tirzepatide Works

  • GLP-1 receptor activation — Suppresses appetite, slows gastric emptying, improves insulin secretion.
  • GIP receptor activation — Enhances fat metabolism, improves insulin sensitivity in adipose tissue, and may help mitigate nausea.
  • Synergistic effects — The two hormones working together produce greater metabolic improvements than either alone.
Why dual action matters: GIP was initially thought to contribute to obesity, but research revealed that at pharmacological doses, GIP activation enhances fat burning and works synergistically with GLP-1 for superior outcomes.

Clinical Trial Results

SURMOUNT Trials (Weight Management)

  • SURMOUNT-1: At the highest dose (15 mg), participants lost an average of 22.5% of body weight over 72 weeks — with over a third losing 25%+.
  • SURMOUNT-2 (type 2 diabetes): Average weight loss of 14.7% at highest dose.
  • SURMOUNT-3 (after intensive lifestyle intervention): Average weight loss of 26.6% — the highest ever for a non-surgical intervention.
  • SURMOUNT-4 (withdrawal study): Patients who stopped regained significant weight, reinforcing the need for continued treatment.

SURPASS Trials (Diabetes)

Showed superior A1C reduction compared to semaglutide, insulin, and other treatments, with up to 2.4% A1C reduction from baseline.

Head-to-head: In SURPASS-2, tirzepatide 15 mg achieved significantly greater A1C reduction and weight loss compared to semaglutide 1 mg (Ozempic dose).

Side Effects

Very Common (>10%)

  • Nausea (~29%) — generally milder than semaglutide
  • Diarrhoea (~23%)
  • Decreased appetite (~20%)
  • Vomiting (~12%)
  • Constipation (~11%)

Serious (Rare)

  • Pancreatitis
  • Gallbladder disease
  • Thyroid C-cell tumours (boxed warning)
  • Hypoglycemia (with insulin/sulfonylureas)
