10 Quick High-Protein Breakfasts for Low Appetite Days

Updated Feb 2026·8 min read

One of the most common challenges on GLP-1 medications is breakfast. Your appetite is suppressed, the thought of a full plate feels overwhelming, and yet you know you need protein to preserve muscle mass and stay energised. Skipping meals entirely leads to muscle loss, fatigue, and nutrient deficiencies — none of which help your long-term results.

The solution isn't forcing down a big meal. It's finding small, protein-dense options that go down easily even when you're not hungry. Every idea below delivers 20–40g of protein, takes 10 minutes or less, and works well on a sensitive stomach.

Why protein matters on GLP-1s: When you're eating significantly less food, your body can break down muscle along with fat. Research suggests aiming for 60–100g of protein per day (or roughly 1.2–1.5g per kg of body weight) to minimise muscle loss. Getting 25–30g at breakfast sets a strong foundation for the day.

1. Two-ingredient protein pancakes

Mash one ripe banana with two eggs, mix, and cook like small pancakes in a non-stick pan. That's it — no flour, no fuss. The banana provides natural sweetness and the eggs provide the protein. Top with a tablespoon of nut butter for extra protein and healthy fats.

~20g protein · 5 minutes · gentle on the stomach

2. Greek yoghurt protein bowl

Start with 200g of full-fat or 2% Greek yoghurt (choose a brand with at least 15g protein per serving). Add a scoop of unflavoured or vanilla protein powder, a handful of berries, and a drizzle of honey. The texture stays creamy rather than chalky if you stir the protein powder in thoroughly. This is one of the easiest breakfasts to eat when appetite is low because it's cold, smooth, and doesn't have a strong smell.

~35g protein · 3 minutes · no cooking required

3. Egg muffin cups (batch prep)

Whisk 6 eggs with a splash of milk, diced ham or turkey, and whatever vegetables you like — spinach, peppers, and mushrooms all work well. Pour into a greased muffin tin and bake at 180°C (350°F) for 18–20 minutes. Makes 6 muffin cups that keep in the fridge for 4 days. Eat 2–3 for breakfast, cold or reheated in 30 seconds.

~25g protein (3 cups) · 5 min prep, 20 min bake · great for meal prep

4. Cottage cheese toast

Spread a generous layer of cottage cheese on a piece of wholegrain toast. The trend of whipped cottage cheese exists for a reason — it's incredibly protein-dense for how light it feels. Top with everything bagel seasoning for a savoury version, or sliced strawberries and a drizzle of honey for sweet. One thick slice of toast with 150g of cottage cheese delivers serious protein with minimal effort.

~22g protein · 3 minutes · no cooking required

5. Protein smoothie (the reliable fallback)

When you absolutely cannot face solid food, a smoothie is your best friend. Blend one scoop of protein powder, one cup of milk (dairy or a high-protein plant milk like soy), half a frozen banana, a tablespoon of peanut butter, and a handful of ice. Sipping slowly over 30–45 minutes is much easier than sitting down to a meal, and you can take it with you.

~35g protein · 3 minutes · easiest option on nausea days

Smoothie tip for nausea: Add a small piece of fresh ginger (about 1cm) to your smoothie. Ginger has well-documented anti-nausea properties and blends right into the flavour. Keeping the smoothie cold and sipping slowly also helps — avoid gulping.

6. Overnight oats with protein

Combine 40g of rolled oats, one scoop of protein powder, 150ml of milk, and a tablespoon of chia seeds in a jar. Stir, seal, and refrigerate overnight. In the morning, stir again and top with berries or sliced banana. The texture is soft and cold — much more appealing on a suppressed appetite than hot porridge. The chia seeds add fibre and omega-3s.

~30g protein · 5 min prep (night before) · zero morning effort

7. Smoked salmon roll-ups

Lay out slices of smoked salmon, spread a thin layer of cream cheese on each, and roll them up. That's the whole recipe. You can add capers, dill, or a squeeze of lemon if you want, but the basic version is already a high-protein, low-volume breakfast that's easy to eat in small bites. Three roll-ups take about 2 minutes to assemble.

~25g protein (3 roll-ups) · 2 minutes · no cooking, no heat, no strong smells

8. Turkey and cheese wrap

Take a small wholegrain wrap, layer on 3–4 slices of turkey breast and a slice of cheese, add a handful of spinach if you're feeling ambitious, and roll it up. Cut it in half so it feels more manageable. This works well as something you can eat in stages — half now, half in an hour — rather than all at once.

~28g protein · 3 minutes · easily portable

9. Scrambled eggs with ricotta

Crack 2–3 eggs into a bowl, add two tablespoons of ricotta cheese, and scramble gently over low heat. The ricotta makes the eggs incredibly soft and creamy — a completely different texture from standard scrambled eggs. Season with salt, pepper, and chives. The soft texture is particularly good when your stomach is sensitive, and the ricotta bumps up the protein without adding volume.

~25g protein · 5 minutes · gentle texture

10. Edamame and egg rice bowl

If you have leftover rice (or 90-second microwave rice), warm a small portion and top with a soft-boiled or fried egg, a handful of shelled edamame, a drizzle of soy sauce, and a sprinkle of sesame seeds. This is more of a meal than the other options, so it works best on days when your appetite is slightly better but you still want something quick. The combination of egg, edamame, and rice provides complete protein.

~22g protein · 5 minutes · savoury and satisfying

General tips for eating breakfast on GLP-1 medications

Eat within an hour of waking. Your appetite may not return later in the morning — if anything, GLP-1 medications tend to suppress it more as the day goes on. Getting protein in early gives you the best chance of hitting your daily target.

Start small. You don't have to finish everything. Eating two-thirds of any of these breakfasts is still better than eating nothing. Give yourself permission to stop when you're full.

Cold foods are often easier. Many people on GLP-1s find that cold or room-temperature foods are more tolerable than hot meals, partly because they produce less smell. Greek yoghurt, smoothies, overnight oats, and smoked salmon all work well for this reason.

Protein first, everything else second. If you can only eat a few bites, make sure those bites are the protein. Eat the eggs before the toast, the salmon before the cream cheese. When your total food intake is limited, every bite needs to count.

Don't drink large amounts of water right before eating. GLP-1 medications already slow gastric emptying. A full glass of water before breakfast can make you feel uncomfortably full before you've eaten anything. Sip water between meals instead.

Batch prep is your friend. On a good appetite day, make a batch of egg muffin cups or overnight oats for the week. On a bad day, you'll be glad the work is already done.
