The 8 Best Protein Shakes for GLP-1 Patients, Ranked

Updated Apr 2026·10 min read

Getting enough protein on a GLP-1 medication is genuinely hard. Your appetite is suppressed, your stomach empties slowly, and food — especially dense, protein-rich food — can feel unappealing or even nauseating. Meanwhile, the research is clear: up to 25–40% of the weight lost on GLP-1 medications can be lean muscle rather than fat, and protein is the primary tool for preventing that.

Protein shakes fill the gap. On days when you can't face a chicken breast or don't have room for a full meal, a well-chosen shake can deliver 25–40g of protein in a format you can actually get down. The problem is that most protein shakes weren't designed with GLP-1 patients in mind — they're too thick, too sweet, too large in volume, or full of additives that irritate an already-sensitive digestive system.

Here's what actually works, ranked by how well they hold up to the specific demands of life on Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or Saxenda.

What to look for in a GLP-1-friendly shake: 20–40g protein per serving, under 250 calories, low fat (fat slows gastric emptying further), low sugar, minimal additives and thickeners, and a volume you can realistically finish. Texture matters more than most people expect — thick, gummy shakes are much harder to tolerate than smooth or clear ones.

A note on how we ranked these

We evaluated each option across four criteria: protein content and quality, digestibility on a suppressed appetite, taste and texture, and value. We've prioritised ready-to-drink options for convenience, but included top powder options where they offer meaningful advantages. These aren't sponsored rankings — affiliate links are labelled where present.

1. Fairlife Core Power (42g protein) — Best overall

Fairlife Core Power has become something close to the unofficial protein shake of the GLP-1 community, and the reasons are obvious. At 42g of protein per 14oz bottle, it delivers more than almost any other ready-to-drink option. The protein comes from ultra-filtered milk, which removes most of the lactose — meaning it's easier on the gut than standard dairy shakes. The texture is smooth and genuinely milk-like rather than thick or gluey, which makes it far more tolerable on a queasy day.

At around 230 calories, it's substantial enough to serve as a meal replacement on low-appetite days. Flavours include chocolate, vanilla, strawberry, and chocolate peanut butter — the chocolate is widely considered the best. The main downside is cost: around £3–4 per bottle in the UK and $3–4 in the US, which adds up if you're using them daily.

Best for: High-appetite-suppression days when you need real nutrition in minimal volume. Meal replacement on injection days.

2. Whey protein isolate powder (DIY) — Best value

If you're going to use protein shakes regularly, a good whey protein isolate powder mixed with milk or water gives you the most control at the lowest cost per gram of protein. Whey isolate is the gold standard for GLP-1 patients: it's rapidly absorbed, high in essential amino acids (particularly leucine, which signals muscle protein synthesis), and very low in lactose compared to whey concentrate.

Top-rated isolates include MyProtein Impact Whey Isolate, Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard 100% Whey, and Bulk Pure Whey Isolate. Look for products with minimal ingredients — the fewer thickeners, gums, and artificial additives, the gentler on a sensitive stomach. Mix with cold water or semi-skimmed milk and drink it cold; warm or room-temperature shakes are significantly harder to tolerate on GLP-1 medications.

Best for: Regular daily use, post-workout recovery, anyone who finds pre-made shakes too sweet or too expensive.

3. Ensure Max Protein (30g protein) — Best for nausea days

Ensure Max Protein is clinically formulated as a medical nutrition supplement, which means it's designed to be easy to digest and nutritionally complete. Each 330ml bottle contains 30g of protein, 150 calories, and a full range of vitamins and minerals — useful for patients who aren't eating much and are at risk of micronutrient deficiencies.

The texture is thinner than Fairlife and significantly less sweet than many protein shakes, which makes it more manageable on days when anything rich or intense triggers nausea. The vanilla and café mocha flavours are the most neutral. It's widely available in pharmacies and supermarkets in both the UK and US, which is a practical advantage.

Best for: Days of significant nausea, patients who are also managing nutritional deficiencies, older patients.

4. Clear whey isolate (powder) — Best for texture sensitivity

Clear whey is a relatively new category that's become increasingly popular with GLP-1 patients who can't tolerate traditional creamy shakes. Rather than mixing into a thick milky drink, clear whey dissolves into a juice-like liquid — lighter, more refreshing, and dramatically easier to drink when your stomach is unsettled.

MyProtein Clear Whey Isolate and Bulk Clear Whey Isolate are both well-regarded options. Flavours like lemon, peach, and watermelon work better than anything chocolate or vanilla in this format. Protein content is typically 20–25g per serving, lower than standard whey isolate, but the key advantage is that many GLP-1 patients who struggle with creamy shakes can finish a clear whey drink without difficulty.

Best for: Anyone who finds traditional protein shakes too heavy, too sweet, or nauseating. Early weeks of treatment or after dose increases.

5. Huel Black Edition (40g protein) — Best complete nutrition

Huel Black Edition is a meal replacement rather than a pure protein shake, but its nutritional profile makes it worth including. Each serving delivers 40g of plant-based protein (from pea and brown rice), 400 calories, and a complete micronutrient profile including vitamin D, B12, iron, and omega-3s — all the nutrients that GLP-1 patients commonly fall short on when eating less.

The texture is thicker than whey-based options, which puts it lower on the list for nausea tolerance. Mix with extra water to thin it out. It works best on days when your appetite is low but you have the capacity to eat something substantial, and you need one solution that covers protein, calories, and micronutrients simultaneously. The chocolate and vanilla flavours are the most palatable.

Best for: Patients who are significantly under-eating and need a nutritionally complete meal replacement rather than a protein top-up.

6. Pure Protein Ready to Drink (35g protein) — Best budget RTD

Pure Protein's ready-to-drink shakes are one of the more affordable pre-made options at around $2–2.50 per bottle in the US (less widely available in the UK). At 35g protein and 150 calories in an 11oz serving, the nutrition-to-volume ratio is excellent. The texture lands somewhere between Fairlife and a standard protein shake — smooth enough for most days, though some find it slightly sweet.

Flavours include chocolate, chocolate peanut butter, salted caramel, strawberry, and vanilla. The chocolate is consistently rated the best-tasting, and the smaller serving size makes it more manageable to finish than larger bottles on low-appetite days.

Best for: Budget-conscious patients in the US who want a solid ready-to-drink option without paying premium prices.

7. Orgain Organic Protein (21g protein) — Best plant-based

For patients who are dairy-free, vegan, or who find whey-based shakes trigger digestive issues, Orgain Organic Protein is the most consistently well-tolerated plant-based option. It uses a blend of pea, brown rice, and chia seed protein — which together form a more complete amino acid profile than any single plant source alone.

At 21g protein per serving it's lower than the whey options, but the cleaner ingredient list and plant-based base make it easier on sensitive stomachs. The chocolate fudge flavour is smooth and not overly sweet. Available in both ready-to-drink and powder formats.

Best for: Vegan patients, those with dairy sensitivity, anyone who finds whey protein triggering GI symptoms.

8. Protein shots (20g in 60ml) — Best when volume is the problem

Protein shots — concentrated 60ml servings delivering 15–20g of protein — are the extreme low-volume option for patients who simply cannot get down a standard shake. Brands like Shots Nutrition, PhD Nutrition, and For Goodness Shakes make shots that can be downed in seconds.

The trade-off is taste: protein shots are often intensely flavoured and can leave a strong aftertaste. They're not a daily staple for most people, but they're a useful emergency option when you've eaten almost nothing all day and need to at least hit a minimum protein threshold.

Best for: Severe nausea days, travelling, or as a quick top-up when you've fallen short of your protein target by end of day.

What to avoid

A few categories that tend to cause problems for GLP-1 patients:

  • Mass gainers. Designed for people trying to eat more, not less. High in carbohydrates and calories you don't need, and too large a volume to be practical.
  • Casein-only shakes. Casein is a slow-digesting milk protein that forms a gel in the stomach. Combined with GLP-1 medications already slowing gastric emptying, it can cause significant discomfort and nausea. Fine occasionally but not ideal as a daily staple.
  • Shakes heavy in gums and thickeners. Xanthan gum, guar gum, and carrageenan are common protein shake additives that can cause bloating and digestive distress — worse on a GLP-1 medication. Check the ingredients list.
  • Collagen-only products. Collagen is an incomplete protein — it lacks tryptophan and has an inferior amino acid profile for muscle preservation. Fine as a supplement but shouldn't be your primary protein source.
  • High-sugar shakes. Anything over 10–15g of sugar per serving can cause rapid blood sugar fluctuations, which interact badly with GLP-1 medications and can worsen nausea.

How to make shakes more tolerable

Even the best shake can be hard to get down on a bad day. A few strategies that help:

  • Serve cold. Ice-cold shakes are significantly easier to tolerate than room-temperature ones. Keep them in the fridge, or pour over ice.
  • Sip slowly. Drinking too fast overloads a stomach that's already emptying slowly. Give yourself 20–30 minutes.
  • Dilute if needed. Adding extra water to a powder shake reduces sweetness and volume simultaneously — both helpful when tolerance is low.
  • Try different flavours. Taste sensitivity often changes on GLP-1 medications. Something you loved before might be off-putting now. Chocolate doesn't suit everyone; unflavoured or fruit-based options are worth trying.
  • Time it right. Many patients find the day after injection the hardest. If that's the case for you, front-load your protein intake on days 3–7 of the cycle when tolerance is better.
The protein maths: Most GLP-1 patients should aim for 80–120g of protein daily, depending on body weight and activity level. A single shake delivering 25–40g of protein covers a meaningful portion of that — the equivalent of two to three eggs, or a large chicken breast. On days when solid food isn't happening, two shakes can form the backbone of your protein intake.

The bottom line

Fairlife Core Power is the best ready-to-drink option for most people — high protein, smooth texture, genuinely good taste. Whey protein isolate powder is the best value for regular daily use. Clear whey is the best option if texture is your primary challenge. And protein shots are there for the hardest days when volume is the issue.

No shake will suit everyone — GLP-1 patients vary enormously in what they can tolerate, and that tolerance often changes week to week. Start with small volumes, experiment with temperature and texture, and treat shakes as the practical tool they are: not a replacement for eating well, but a reliable way to hit your protein target on the days when eating well isn't possible.
