Meal Plan

The 1,200–1,500 Calorie GLP-1 Weekly Meal Plan

Updated Feb 2026·12 min read

Most meal plans aren't designed for people on GLP-1 medications. They assume a normal appetite, normal portion sizes, and the ability to eat three full meals a day. On Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or Zepbound, reality looks very different — smaller windows of appetite, food aversions that come and go, and a stomach that fills up fast.

This plan is built specifically for that reality. It targets 1,200–1,500 calories and 80–100g of protein per day across three small meals and one optional snack. Every meal is simple, quick, and designed to go down easily even on tough appetite days. There's a shopping list at the end.

A note on calories: 1,200–1,500 calories is a common range for GLP-1 patients, but your needs may differ based on your starting weight, activity level, and how far along you are in treatment. If you're consistently eating under 1,000 calories, talk to your doctor — you may need to adjust your dose or add a nutritional supplement. The goal is sustainable fat loss while preserving muscle, not extreme restriction.

How the plan works

Each day follows a simple structure: a protein-focused breakfast, a balanced lunch, a satisfying dinner, and one optional snack for when appetite allows. Protein is prioritised at every meal. Meals are designed to be interchangeable — if you don't fancy Wednesday's lunch, swap it with any other day's lunch. The plan also repeats some ingredients across the week to keep the shopping list manageable.

Monday

~1,350 cal · ~90g protein

Breakfast — Greek yoghurt protein bowl. 200g Greek yoghurt, one scoop vanilla protein powder, handful of blueberries, drizzle of honey. Stir the protein powder in well. (35g protein, ~300 cal)

Lunch — Tuna salad lettuce wraps. Mix one tin of tuna (drained) with a tablespoon of Greek yoghurt, a squeeze of lemon, diced celery, salt and pepper. Spoon into large lettuce leaves and wrap. (30g protein, ~200 cal)

Dinner — Lemon herb chicken with roasted vegetables. Bake one chicken breast with lemon juice, garlic, and dried herbs at 200°C (400°F) for 20 minutes. Serve with a small portion of roasted courgette and red pepper. (35g protein, ~350 cal)

Snack — Apple slices with almond butter. Half an apple with one tablespoon of almond butter. (4g protein, ~150 cal)

Tuesday

~1,300 cal · ~88g protein

Breakfast — Two-ingredient protein pancakes. Mash one banana with two eggs, cook as small pancakes. Top with a tablespoon of peanut butter. (20g protein, ~300 cal)

Lunch — Turkey and cheese wrap. Small wholegrain wrap with 3–4 slices of turkey breast, one slice of cheese, handful of spinach, mustard. (28g protein, ~300 cal)

Dinner — Prawn stir-fry. Sauté 150g prawns with broccoli, snap peas, and a splash of soy sauce and sesame oil. Serve over a small portion of rice (60g cooked). (30g protein, ~350 cal)

Snack — Cottage cheese with cucumber. 100g cottage cheese with sliced cucumber and everything bagel seasoning. (12g protein, ~100 cal)

Wednesday

~1,400 cal · ~95g protein

Breakfast — Overnight oats with protein. Made the night before: 40g rolled oats, one scoop protein powder, 150ml milk, tablespoon of chia seeds. Top with sliced strawberries in the morning. (30g protein, ~350 cal)

Lunch — Egg muffin cups (×3) with side salad. Three pre-made egg muffin cups (see breakfast guide) reheated, with a small side salad dressed with olive oil and lemon. (25g protein, ~250 cal)

Dinner — Baked salmon with sweet potato. Bake one salmon fillet at 200°C for 12–15 minutes. Serve with half a small baked sweet potato and steamed green beans. (30g protein, ~400 cal)

Snack — String cheese and a handful of grapes. (8g protein, ~120 cal)

Thursday

~1,250 cal · ~85g protein

Breakfast — Scrambled eggs with ricotta. Three eggs scrambled with two tablespoons of ricotta cheese over low heat. Season with chives, salt, and pepper. (25g protein, ~280 cal)

Lunch — Chicken and avocado bowl. 100g leftover chicken (from Monday or batch-cooked), sliced over mixed greens with half an avocado, cherry tomatoes, and a light vinaigrette. (28g protein, ~350 cal)

Dinner — Turkey meatballs in marinara. Form 150g turkey mince into small meatballs, bake at 200°C for 15 minutes, and simmer in shop-bought marinara sauce. Serve over a small portion of pasta or with a piece of crusty bread. (32g protein, ~400 cal)

Snack — Protein smoothie. If appetite allows: protein powder, milk, half a banana, ice. Skip if you're full from dinner. (~25g protein, ~220 cal)

Friday

~1,350 cal · ~82g protein

Breakfast — Smoked salmon roll-ups. Three slices of smoked salmon spread with cream cheese, rolled up. Add capers or dill if you like. (25g protein, ~250 cal)

Lunch — Lentil soup. A bowl of ready-made or homemade lentil soup (red lentil works well — it's smooth and easy to eat). Top with a squeeze of lemon and a drizzle of olive oil. (15g protein, ~250 cal)

Dinner — Steak with roasted broccoli. Pan-sear a small sirloin steak (150g) for 3–4 minutes per side. Rest for 5 minutes. Serve with roasted broccoli tossed in olive oil, garlic, and a pinch of chilli flakes. (35g protein, ~400 cal)

Snack — Greek yoghurt with honey. 150g Greek yoghurt with a drizzle of honey. (12g protein, ~150 cal)

Saturday

~1,400 cal · ~92g protein

Breakfast — Cottage cheese toast. One slice of wholegrain toast topped with 150g cottage cheese and everything bagel seasoning. (22g protein, ~250 cal)

Lunch — Chicken quesadilla. One small wholegrain wrap with shredded chicken, a sprinkle of cheese, and a tablespoon of salsa. Cook in a dry pan until the cheese melts and the wrap is crispy. Cut into triangles. (30g protein, ~350 cal)

Dinner — Mediterranean cod. Bake one cod fillet at 200°C for 12 minutes topped with diced tomatoes, olives, capers, and a drizzle of olive oil. Serve with a small portion of couscous. (30g protein, ~400 cal)

Snack — Edamame. A small bowl of shelled edamame with a sprinkle of sea salt. (10g protein, ~120 cal)

Sunday

~1,350 cal · ~90g protein

Breakfast — Veggie omelette. Three-egg omelette with spinach, mushrooms, and a sprinkle of feta cheese. (25g protein, ~300 cal)

Lunch — Smoked salmon and avocado toast. One slice of wholegrain toast with mashed avocado and a few slices of smoked salmon. Squeeze of lemon, pinch of black pepper. (20g protein, ~300 cal)

Dinner — Slow cooker chicken thighs. Place 2 bone-in chicken thighs in a slow cooker with tinned tomatoes, chickpeas, garlic, cumin, and paprika. Cook on low for 6 hours. Protein-rich, minimal effort, and the leftovers keep well. (35g protein, ~400 cal)

Snack — Dark chocolate and almonds. Two squares of dark chocolate with a small handful of almonds. (5g protein, ~150 cal)

  • Salmon fillet (×1) and cod fillet (×1)
  • Prawns, 150g
  • Small sirloin steak, 150g
  • Turkey breast slices, 1 pack
  • Turkey mince, 150g
  • Smoked salmon, 1 pack
  • Tinned tuna, 1 tin
  • Eggs, 1 dozen

Dairy & protein powder

  • Cottage cheese, 300g
  • Ricotta cheese, small tub
  • Feta cheese, small block
  • Sliced cheese, 1 pack
  • String cheese, 1 pack

  • Milk, 1 litre
  • Protein powder, vanilla (one tub lasts several weeks)

Fruit & vegetables

Pantry & staples

  • Rolled oats, chia seeds, couscous, rice, small pasta

  • Salt, pepper, chilli flakes

Tips for making this plan work

Batch cook on Sunday. Make the egg muffin cups, overnight oats, and slow cooker chicken all on Sunday. That gives you ready-made meals for several days with minimal weekday effort.

Swap freely. Don't like fish? Swap salmon or cod for an extra chicken meal. Vegetarian some days? Swap the protein for a tin of chickpeas or extra eggs. The structure matters more than the specific recipes.

Skip the snack if you're not hungry. The snacks are optional — they're there to bring you closer to 1,200 calories if your three meals fall short. Don't force them.

Adjust portions to your appetite. Some days you'll eat closer to 1,500 calories, others closer to 1,000. That's normal on GLP-1 medications. Aim for the protein target first, then fill in with healthy carbs and fats as appetite allows.

Keep emergency protein on hand. String cheese, Greek yoghurt cups, protein bars, and pre-cooked chicken strips are all useful for days when cooking feels impossible. Having something ready means you're less likely to skip meals entirely.

Calorie and protein counts are approximate and will vary based on exact brands and portion sizes. This meal plan is for general informational purposes and is not a substitute for personalised dietary advice from a registered dietitian or your healthcare provider.
